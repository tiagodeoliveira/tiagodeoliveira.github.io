# Everyone You Meet Is Going Through Something You Know Nothing About

*Direct is not a synonym for rude. We traded kindness for a false sense of performance, and I think it's time we took it back.*

---

Honestly, I'm tired of hearing "I'm just being direct."

I've heard it in meetings, in code reviews, in performance conversations, in Slack threads that went sideways. Almost every time, it came right after someone said something that was, let's be honest, just rude. I've been in rooms where colleagues walked out emotionally shaken, even disheartened, while the other person kept their posture, proudly holding up the banner of directness. "Direct" has become the badge people pin on disrespect so it looks like a virtue.

Somewhere along the way, especially in business, we decided that politeness is overhead. If I don't "waste time" being nice and go straight to the point, I'm optimizing my time. I'm efficient. I'm high performance. IMO, that's a false sensation of performance, and we as a society have sunk deep into it.

## A spectrum, not a switch

I think about how we communicate with people as a spectrum. Something like this:

![The Candor Spectrum: 1 Disrespectful, 2 Direct, 3 Kind, 4 Sugarcoating, 5 Untrue](/images/candor-spectrum.png)

**1. Disrespectful.** Blunt for the sake of being blunt. Fast, cheap, and careless about the person on the other side.

**2. Direct.** Clear and honest, no detours. And here's the thing: 1 and 2 sit really close to each other. The vast majority of people who claim to be direct are actually being disrespectful and inconsiderate. Not because they're bad people, but because being direct is a *skill*. It takes time to master. It takes experience. When you do it right, you know when it's the moment to be direct and when it's the moment to be something else. When you don't do it properly, you slide straight into 1, and you probably don't even notice.

**3. Kind and considerate.** This is where I want to live most of the time. You can be direct while being respectful. You understand that directness is not binary: sometimes being direct is not the right call, sometimes you need to polish your words. I always say that we don't know what the person in front of us is going through. We don't know what is happening in their lives. Sometimes a few kind words make all the difference. That's empathy, and IMO it's the thing that actually makes all of this work. Before you choose your words, you try to see the moment from the other person's side.

**4. Sugarcoating.** Push kindness further and the message starts to get lost. The feedback is so wrapped in padding that the other person walks away without hearing it.

**5. Untrue.** At the far end, kindness turns into lies. It's no longer softening the message; the communication itself has become untrue. And that's just as disrespectful as 1, only more comfortable for everyone involved.

The point isn't to pick a number and stay there forever. The skill is moving along the spectrum on purpose, knowing which point the moment, and the person, actually needs.

## Why this matters even more now

We work in a globalized world. On any given day, a single team can span different countries, cultures, and backgrounds, and each of them has a different idea of what "direct" means. What sounds efficient to one person sounds harsh to another. What sounds polite to one sounds evasive to another.

That's exactly why "I'm just direct" doesn't cut it. If you don't know who you're talking to, you don't know where on the spectrum your words are landing. Directness without awareness isn't a communication style. It's a gamble with somebody else's day.

## "Be professional" became "stop being a person"

There's another phrase I've grown wary of: "be professional."

Somehow it stopped meaning *do good work and treat people well* and started meaning *stop having feelings*. Leave your emotions outside the office. Be a resource, not a human.

That only works if you can literally split yourself into two people. Nobody can (with the exception of specific mental health conditions). People are people, in business or not. We bring our worries, our wins, our bad nights of sleep, and the worry about a sick dog at the vet to work with us, whether the org chart likes it or not. Pretending otherwise doesn't make us more professional. It just makes us worse at understanding each other.

## Kindness is not overhead

The thing about the "efficiency" argument is that it only counts one side of the ledger. Sure, you saved thirty seconds by skipping the context and the kind words. But what did that cost? A teammate who now hesitates to ask you questions. A junior engineer who spends the evening wondering if they're about to be fired. A partner who quietly stops bringing you bad news early. That's not efficiency. That's technical debt, just in human form.

There's a short video I keep coming back to, [Because Kindness Keeps The World Afloat](https://youtu.be/qQacqJsWyrs). The title says it all. I also have a sticker on my laptop that simply says "be a nice human being". It's a small reminder, but it does its job.

So the next time you're about to say "I'm just being direct", pause for a second. Ask yourself where on the spectrum you really are. Ask what the person in front of you might be going through. You can still say the hard thing. Just say it like a human talking to another human.

Direct is a skill. Kind is a choice. Empathy is what tells you which one the moment needs. And I believe the best communicators practice all three, every single day. :D
